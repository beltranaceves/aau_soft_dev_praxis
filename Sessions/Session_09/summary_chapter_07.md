## Patton (2015) – Chapter 7: Qualitative Interviewing

### Key ideas for preparing good interview questions

### 1. Purpose of qualitative interviewing

Qualitative interviews are used to understand **people’s perspectives, experiences, meanings, and reasoning**—things that cannot be directly observed. The goal is to “enter the interviewee’s world” by asking questions that allow them to speak **in their own words**. The **quality of the data depends heavily on the interviewer’s skill**, openness, and ability to listen.

***

### 2. Three main types of qualitative interviews

1.  **Informal conversational interviews**
    *   No predefined questions
    *   Questions emerge naturally during conversation
    *   Very flexible and context-sensitive
    *   Strength: rich, spontaneous insights
    *   Limitation: hard to compare responses across people

2.  **Interview guide approach** (most relevant for your assignment)
    *   A list of **topics or themes** to be covered
    *   Questions can be phrased differently for each interviewee
    *   Ensures key issues are addressed while allowing flexibility
    *   Ideal for **company visits and group interviews**

3.  **Standardized open-ended interviews**
    *   All questions are written in advance
    *   Asked in the same order and wording to all interviewees
    *   Strength: comparability and easier analysis
    *   Limitation: less flexibility to follow interesting leads

👉 These approaches can be **combined**, e.g. structured questions first, followed by open exploration.

***

### 3. Types of interview questions

Patton highlights that good interview guides include **different kinds of questions**:

*   **Experience / behavior**: What people do or have done  
    *“Can you describe a typical workday here?”*
*   **Opinions / values**: Beliefs, judgments, priorities  
    *“What do you think works particularly well in this organization?”*
*   **Feelings**: Emotional responses  
    *“How did that change affect employees?”*
*   **Knowledge**: What people know or understand  
    *“What does the onboarding process involve?”*
*   **Sensory**: What people see, hear, or notice  
    *“What would a visitor notice when walking through the workplace?”*
*   **Background**: Role, experience, context  
    *“How long have you worked in this role?”*

Good interviews usually **mix several question types**.

***

### 4. Designing good interview questions

**Good qualitative questions should be:**

*   **Open-ended** (not yes/no)
*   **Neutral** (not leading)
*   **Clear and understandable**
*   **Singular** (one issue per question)

**Avoid:**

*   Yes/no questions
*   Leading questions (“Don’t you think…?”)
*   Overloaded questions (“What do you like and dislike and what should change?”)
*   Too many “why” questions (can feel judgmental or hard to answer)

Instead of *“Why did you…?”*, ask:

*   *“What led you to…?”*
*   *“How did that come about?”*

***

### 5. Sequencing the interview

Patton recommends starting with:

1.  **Easy, descriptive questions** (present experiences)
2.  **Opinions and feelings**
3.  **More reflective or sensitive topics**
4.  **Background questions**, preferably near the end

This helps build rapport and keeps the interview flowing naturally.

***

### 6. Interviewing skills that matter

A good interviewer:

*   Builds **rapport** (respect, interest, empathy)
*   Maintains **neutrality** (no judgment of answers)
*   Listens more than talks
*   Uses **probes** to get richer answers:
    *   *“Can you tell me more about that?”*
    *   *“Could you give an example?”*
*   Keeps the interview focused but flexible

***

### 7. Recording and note-taking

*   Recording the interview (with permission) is ideal
*   Notes should capture **key points, phrases, and potential follow-ups**
*   After the interview, reflect: What stood out? What needs clarification?

***

### 8. Ethical considerations

Interviews affect people. Researchers must:

*   Explain the purpose of the interview clearly
*   Obtain informed consent
*   Respect confidentiality
*   Allow people to skip questions
*   Be sensitive when topics become emotional or personal

***

### 9. Key takeaway for your company visit

For your interview guide:

*   Use the **interview guide approach**
*   Prepare **themes**, not rigid scripts
*   Ask open, neutral, experience-based questions
*   Be ready to **listen and adapt**
*   Focus on understanding how the company looks and feels **from the interviewee’s perspective**
