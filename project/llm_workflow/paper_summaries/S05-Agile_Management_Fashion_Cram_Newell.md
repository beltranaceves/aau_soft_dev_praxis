# S05: Mindful Revolution or Mindless Trend? Agile as a Management Fashion

## Source
Cram, W. A., & Newell, S. (2016). Mindful revolution or mindless trend? Examining agile development as a management fashion. *European Journal of Information Systems*, 25(2), 154-169.

## Core Idea
Agile development adoption can be understood through **management fashion theory** — not to judge it as good/bad, but to understand how organizations mindfully or mindlessly adopt it.

## Five Management Fashion Indicators

| Indicator | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **1. Norm of Progress** | Adopted because it's seen as NOVEL — cutting-edge, advanced |
| **2. Norm of Rationality** | Adopted because OTHERS (gurus, experts) say it's the most efficient approach |
| **3. Socio-psychological Forces** | Adopted because it fulfills employees' psychological needs (creativity, individuality, status) |
| **4. Techno-economic Forces** | Adopted because it fits the organization's culture, structure, and economic context |
| **5. Innovation Devolution** | Adopted ceremonially — the original intent "devolves" into ambiguous practices that provide legitimacy but little real value |

## Three Categories of Agile Adoption

### Crusader
- **Adoption**: Pure, prescriptive, "by-the-book" agile
- **Other approaches**: None — exclusively agile
- **Mindfulness**: High — but risk of mindlessness if context doesn't fit
- **Fashion indicators**: Low norm of progress, high rationality, high socio-psych, high techno-economic, low devolution
- **Example**: CodeCorp (pure XP)
- **Risk**: May be mindless if organizational circumstances actually require hybrid approach

### Tailor
- **Adoption**: Adopts most agile techniques, modifies on a project-by-project basis
- **Other approaches**: Integrates with traditional where needed
- **Mindfulness**: HIGHEST — continually refining and evaluating effectiveness
- **Fashion indicators**: Moderate across all
- **Example**: LargeMan, InfoOrg (Scrum with customizations)
- **Risk**: Tailoring may deteriorate into innovation devolution

### Dabbler
- **Adoption**: A few ceremonial agile techniques on top of fundamentally traditional approach
- **Other approaches**: Primarily plan-driven
- **Mindfulness**: LOW — mostly mindless; following trend rather than understanding substance
- **Fashion indicators**: High norm of progress, low rationality, low socio-psych, low techno-economic, high devolution
- **Example**: InsureCorp, HealthOrg
- **Risk**: Short-term view; may abandon agile when next trend emerges

## Mindfulness vs. Mindlessness

| Mindful Characteristics | Mindless Characteristics |
|------------------------|------------------------|
| Reluctance to simplify complex situations | Lack of attention to specifics |
| Vigilance in attending to small operational details | Contentedness to be a follower |
| Deference to expertise | Blindly following promises of success stories |
| Commitment to resilience | Minimal investment in long-term success |

## Practical Implications
1. **Crusaders & Tailors** → likely to maintain agile long-term, making it pervasive
2. **Dabblers** → likely short-term, transitory; may abandon agile for next fashion
3. **Fashion ≠ bad**: Even dabblers may gain political/reputational benefits
4. **Counter-fashions**: If agile disappoints (especially for Dabblers), new alternative methods will emerge

## How to Use This in Interview Analysis
1. **Categorize Norlys**: Which category (Crusader/Tailor/Dabbler) best describes Norlys's agile adoption?
2. **Identify fashion indicators**: Look for evidence of the five indicators in interview transcripts
3. **Assess mindfulness**: Are decisions about methods made mindfully (with analysis) or mindlessly (following trends)?
4. **Analyze method adaptation**: How does Norlys customize agile? Is it mindful tailoring or ceremonial dabbling?
5. **Tension between groups**: Different stakeholders may have different views (e.g., management = dabbler, developers = crusader)
