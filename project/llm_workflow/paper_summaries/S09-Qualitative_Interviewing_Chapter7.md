# S09: Qualitative Interviewing (Chapter 7)

## Source
Patton, M. Q. (2002). Qualitative Research & Evaluation Methods (Chapter 7: Qualitative Interviewing). Sage.

## Core Idea
This is a **methodology chapter** about how to conduct qualitative interviews. It covers three types of interviews, types of questions, and techniques for rigorous and skillful interviewing. Essential for understanding how your interview data was collected and how to analyze it.

## The Purpose of Interviewing
"We interview people to find out from them those things we cannot directly observe. We cannot observe feelings, thoughts, and intentions. We cannot observe behaviors that took place at some previous point in time."

**Fundamental principle**: Provide a framework within which respondents can express their own understandings in their own terms.

## Three Types of Qualitative Interviews

| Type | Description | When to Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **1. Informal Conversational** | Questions emerge spontaneously from natural flow of interaction. No predetermined questions. | Fieldwork, participant observation, ethnographic settings |
| **2. Interview Guide Approach** | Topics/issues specified in advance as outline. Interviewer decides wording/sequence during interview. | Most common in research — balances flexibility and systematic coverage |
| **3. Standardized Open-Ended** | Exact wording and sequence predetermined. All respondents asked same questions same way. | Multi-site studies, team research, when comparability is critical |

## Six Types of Questions

| Type | What It Elicits | Example |
|------|----------------|---------|
| **1. Experience/Behavior** | What a person does or has done | "What would I see you doing in a typical day?" |
| **2. Opinion/Values** | Cognitive and interpretive processes | "What do you think about...?" |
| **3. Feeling** | Emotional responses — adjectives | "How do you feel about that?" (look for: anxious, happy, etc.) |
| **4. Knowledge** | Factual information | "What are the eligibility requirements?" |
| **5. Sensory** | What is seen, heard, touched, etc. | "When you walk through the doors, what do you see?" |
| **6. Background/Demographic** | Age, education, occupation, etc. | Open-ended: let people categorize themselves |

### Time Frame Matrix
Questions can be asked in past, present, or future tense — creating 18 possible combinations (6 question types × 3 time frames).

## Wording Questions — Key Criteria

| Criterion | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Truly open-ended** | Doesn't presuppose which dimension will be salient. NOT "How satisfied are you?" (presupposes satisfaction) but "How do you feel about...?" |
| **Avoid dichotomous** | Yes/no questions program the respondent to be reactive. Ask "What happened?" not "Did you have a good time?" |
| **Singular** | One idea per question. Not "How well do you know and like the staff?" |
| **Clear** | Use respondent's language. Avoid labels the interviewee doesn't use. |

## Sequencing Questions
1. **Start with** noncontroversial present behaviors/experiences (easy to answer)
2. **Then** opinions and feelings about those experiences
3. **Then** past and future questions
4. **Background/demographics** — spread throughout or save for end (never start with them!)

## Key Technique: Probing
Follow up on descriptive answers with probes that elicit greater detail. Use respondent's own language.

## How to Use This in Interview Analysis
1. **Understand your data**: Were your Norlys interviews guide-based, conversational, or standardized? This affects what you can claim
2. **Evaluate question quality**: Were questions truly open-ended? Did they avoid dichotomies?
3. **Analyze across question types**: Separate behavior statements from opinions from feelings
4. **Look for emic language**: What terms do Norlys practitioners use among themselves? Use these in your analysis
5. **Apply the same rigor**: When coding interviews, distinguish between what people say they do (behavior) vs. what they think (opinion) vs. what they feel (emotion)
