# S12: Weinberg on Writing — The Fieldstone Method

## Source
Weinberg, G. M. — Chapter 1 ("The Most Important Writing Lesson") and Chapter 2 ("The Fieldstone Method in Brief").

## Core Lesson
**Never attempt to write something you don't care about.**

Weinberg's pivotal moment: In college English composition, his professor (Bill Gaffney) told him he could ignore the class assignments and instead write about whatever he wanted — because Weinberg had written a passionate argument about why he wouldn't do the assignment, and it was the best paper Gaffney had ever received.

## Key Insights
- "Write about what you know" is NOT the rule — Weinberg writes to **learn** about things he cares about
- If you don't care about the topic, you'll struggle ("blood on the forehead")
- If you do care, the work is still hard but not painful
- Example: IBM's NASA proposal — Weinberg couldn't write the assigned proposal because the architecture was wrong, so he wrote an analysis explaining why it wouldn't work, and NASA gave them a sole-source contract

## The Fieldstone Method (Chapter 2)
- Like building a stone wall: gather interesting "stones" (ideas, quotes, observations) one at a time
- Don't worry about structure upfront — just collect material you care about
- Eventually you'll find how the pieces fit together
- **Never be stuck**: if you can't write, gather more stones

## Relevance to Your Exam Paper
1. **Write about what you care about**: Pick a theory that genuinely interests you
2. **Gather your "fieldstones"**: Collect quotes from interview transcripts, concepts from theory, ideas from discussions
3. **Don't struggle**: If you're stuck, you've probably lost the connection to what you care about — step back and reconnect
4. **Passion shows**: The best papers come from genuine interest in the topic

## How to Use This
- Before writing, ask: "What do I really care about in the Norlys data?" and "Which theory fascinates me?"
- Use the Fieldstone Method: collect interview quotes, theoretical concepts, and observations without worrying about structure — the structure will emerge
- When stuck: return to what made you interested in the first place
