# S05: A Paradox Lens to Systems Development Projects — Agile Software Development

## Source
Iivari, J. (2021). A Paradox Lens to Systems Development Projects: The Case of the Agile Software Development. *Communications of the Association for Information Systems*, 49.

## Core Idea
Organizational effectiveness is inherently paradoxical — effective organizations must have simultaneously contradictory attributes. Systems development projects (as temporary organizations) face similar paradoxical tensions. ASD helps address them, but doesn't eliminate them.

## 11 Paradoxical Tensions in Three Groups

### A. Priority Tensions (project goals & performance)

| Tension | Description | How ASD Addresses It |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|
| **1. Quality vs. Quantity (speed)** | Labor-intensive work: you can't maximize both | Requirements prioritization, collective code ownership, technical debt |
| **2. Development Time vs. Development Effort** | Compressing time increases effort (Brooks' law); stretching time increases cost | Time-boxed sprints, small teams (≤9), frequent decision points |
| **3. Efficiency vs. Innovativeness** | Efficiency requires slack-free processes; innovation needs slack resources | Retrospectives support learning; sprint deadlines can kill slack needed for innovation |

**The "Golden Triangle" extended**: Traditional iron triangle (time, cost, quality) is insufficient for ASD. Add: **quantity** (functionality), **innovativeness**, **risk**, and **team satisfaction**.

### B. Structure Tensions

| Tension | Description | ASD Balance |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **4. Management Control vs. Team Autonomy** | Classic tension at all organizational levels | Self-organizing teams + enabling control style (not authoritative) |
| **5. Formality vs. Informality** | Rules/procedures vs. flexible, informal communication | Light methods + minimal documentation; retrospectives allow method adaptation |
| **6. Individual Rewarding vs. Team-based Rewarding** | Individual rewards increase accountability but reduce cooperation; team rewards reduce social loafing but may reduce individual effort | Hybrid rewards recommended; ASD literature advocates team-based rewards |
| **7. Homogeneous vs. Heterogeneous Participants** | Homogeneity eases communication; heterogeneity increases innovation | Cross-functional teams + customer involvement; product owner as single customer voice |

### C. Execution Tensions

| Tension | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **8. Averse vs. Responsive to Requirements Changes** | Traditional methods vs. agile's "welcome changing requirements" |
| **9. Blueprint Planning vs. Continuous Planning** | Upfront fixed plan vs. adaptive planning; ASD uses continuous planning but still needs some upfront architecture/architecture work |
| **10. Rigid Method Enactment vs. Flexible Method Enactment** | Following method literally vs. adapting on the fly; ASD has built-in flexibility |
| **11. Disciplined Process vs. Spontaneous Process** | Following prescribed practices vs. improvisation; ASD allows both |

## Key Takeaway
ASD does not eliminate these tensions — it provides **mechanisms to address them** (retrospectives, prioritization, self-organization). The tensions persist and require **human ingenuity and judgement** to navigate.

## How to Use This in Interview Analysis
1. **Identify tensions**: Which paradoxical tensions are visible in Norlys? Where do stakeholders disagree about priorities (quality vs. speed, control vs. autonomy)?
2. **Analyze how Norlys addresses tensions**: Do they use ASD mechanisms (retrospectives, prioritization) effectively?
3. **Look for unresolved tensions**: Where are tensions causing problems because they're not being addressed?
4. **Use as diagnostic framework**: The 11 tensions can be used as a checklist in retrospectives or interview coding
