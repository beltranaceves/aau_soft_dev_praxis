# S03: Mintzberg's Organization Theory — Structure in Fives

## Source
Mintzberg, H. (1989). *Mintzberg on Management: Inside Our Strange World of Organizations*, pp. 98-115. Free Press.

## Overview
A configurational theory of organization structure: limited number of configurations explain most of what can be observed in organizations. Structure = how labor is divided into tasks + how coordination is achieved.

## Six Basic Parts of an Organization
| Part | What They Do |
|------|-------------|
| **Strategic Apex** | Top management — oversees the whole system |
| **Middle Line** | Managers between apex and operating core |
| **Operating Core** | Operators who produce products/render services |
| **Technostructure** | Analysts who plan and control work (staff functions) |
| **Support Staff** | Internal services (cafeteria, legal, PR, etc.) |
| **Ideology (Culture)** | Traditions, beliefs that distinguish the organization |

Also: **External Coalition** — outside influencers (owners, unions, suppliers, clients, competitors, government)

## Six Coordinating Mechanisms (the "glue" that holds orgs together)
Ordered from simplest to most complex:

1. **Mutual adjustment** — informal communication (simplest AND most complex)
2. **Direct supervision** — one person issues orders to others
3. **Standardization of work processes** — specifying how work is done (rules, procedures)
4. **Standardization of outputs** — specifying results (targets, specifications)
5. **Standardization of skills/knowledge** — related training (e.g., surgeons)
6. **Standardization of norms** — same beliefs across org (e.g., religious order)

**Key insight**: As work becomes more complex, coordination shifts from mutual adjustment → direct supervision → standardization → back to mutual adjustment (for the most complex work).

## Design Parameters
- **Job specialization** — horizontal (# of tasks) and vertical (control over tasks)
- **Behavior formalization** — standardization via rules (bureaucratic vs organic)
- **Training** — standardization of skills (substitute for formalization)
- **Indoctrination** — standardization of norms (substitute for formalization)
- **Unit grouping** — by function (process) or market (product/client)
- **Unit size (span of control)** — small when mutual adjustment needed; large when work is standardized
- **Planning & control systems** — action planning (specific results) vs performance control (range of results)
- **Liaison devices** — positions, task forces, matrix structures to encourage mutual adjustment
- **Decentralization** — vertical (down hierarchy) and horizontal (to non-managers); parallel vs selective

## Situational/Contingency Factors

### Age & Size
- Older org → more formalized behavior
- Larger org → more formalized, more elaborate structure, more specialization
- Structure reflects age of the industry

### Technical System
- Regulating technical system → bureaucratic operating core
- Complex technical system → elaborate professional support staff
- Automation of operating core → organic administrative structure

### Environment
- Dynamic environment → organic structure
- Complex environment → decentralized structure
- Diversified markets → divisionalized structure
- Extreme hostility → centralized structure

### Power
- External control → centralized and formalized structure
- Divided external coalition → politicized internal coalition
- Fashion favors current structural trends (often inappropriate)

## Seven Organizational Configurations

| Configuration | Prime Coordinating Mechanism | Key Part | Type of Decentralization | Pull |
|--------------|------------------------------|----------|--------------------------|------|
| **Entrepreneurial** | Direct supervision | Strategic apex | Vertical & horizontal centralization | To lead |
| **Machine** | Standardization of work processes | Technostructure | Limited horizontal decentralization | To rationalize |
| **Professional** | Standardization of skills | Operating core | Horizontal decentralization | To professionalize |
| **Diversified** | Standardization of outputs | Middle line | Limited vertical decentralization | To balkanize |
| **Innovative (Adhocracy)** | Mutual adjustment | Support staff | Selective decentralization | To collaborate |
| **Missionary** | Standardization of norms | Ideology | Decentralization | To pull together |
| **Political** | None | None | Varies | To pull apart |

### Key Descriptions
- **Entrepreneurial**: Strategic apex over operating core directly, little else. Simple, centralized.
- **Machine**: Fully elaborated line and staff structure, controlling and protecting operating core. Bureaucratic.
- **Professional**: Small technostructure/middle line, large support staff. Professionals control their own work.
- **Diversified**: HQ oversees divisions (usually machine configurations within).
- **Innovative (Adhocracy)**: Multidisciplinary teams, fluid roles, matrix structure. Best for sophisticated innovation.
- **Missionary**: Pure decentralization — trusted members decide for overall good.
- **Political**: Conflict dominates; no stable form.

## How to Use This in Interview Analysis
1. **Identify the configuration(s)** at Norlys — What coordinating mechanism dominates? Which part is key?
2. **Analyze via situational factors** — Size? Age? Environment (dynamic/stable, complex/simple)? Technical system?
3. **Look for tensions** between parts (e.g., technostructure pulling to rationalize while operating core pulls to professionalize)
4. **Examine coordination mechanisms** — How is work coordinated? Mutual adjustment (agile teams)? Standardization (regulations, compliance)?
5. **Assess formalization vs organic structure** — Are there many rules/procedures or flexible teams?
6. **Growing organizations** (like Norlys may be) tend to formalize (Brandborg's finding) — look for this tension
