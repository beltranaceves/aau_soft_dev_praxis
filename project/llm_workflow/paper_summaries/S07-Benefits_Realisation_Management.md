# S07: Factors Affecting Successful Realisation of Benefits from IS Development

## Source
Doherty, N. F., Ashurst, C., & Peppard, J. (2012). Factors affecting the successful realisation of benefits from systems development projects. *Journal of Information Technology*, 27, 1-16.

## Core Idea
IT projects still fail at high rates despite known success factors. The problem is that success is measured by **on-time, on-budget delivery of a technical artifact**, not by **delivery of meaningful business benefits**. Organizations should adopt **Benefits Realisation Management (BRM)** — proactively managing IT investments to ensure they deliver benefits.

## Key Statistics
- 1970s: only 20% of projects achieved intended benefits
- 1990s: up to 90% failed to meet goals
- 2000s: 74% failed to deliver expected value; only ~16% truly successful
- 24% outright failures, 44% "challenged" (late, over budget, fewer features)

## The Core Problem: "Failed Success"
A project may deliver on time, to specification, within budget — but still not deliver real business benefits. Nelson (2005) calls this a **"failed success"**.

## Three Case Studies

| Organization | Approach | Outcome |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| **A: Health Authority** | Trying BRM but imposed on existing projects | FAILED — benefits planning "force-fitted"; no real benefits delivered |
| **B: University** | No explicit benefits focus | MIXED — delivery OK but no organizational change to realize benefits |
| **C: City Council** | Explicit BRM through Transformation Programme | **SUCCESS** — projects delivered on time AND achieved benefits (CRM: 70%→83% first-contact resolution; HR system reduced sickness absence) |

## Six Traditional Success Factors → Benefits-Oriented Version

| Traditional Factor | Benefits-Oriented Version |
|-------------------|--------------------------|
| **1. Clear goals & objectives** | → **Detailed benefits planning** (e.g., Benefits Dependency Network) |
| **2. Project management** | → **Management of transformation** (technology + organizational change) |
| **3. Well-balanced project teams** | → **Coherent governance structures** (project board, vision group, operational reps) |
| **4. Senior management support** | → **Active business leadership** (CEOs personally leading, daily involvement) |
| **5. User participation** | → **Stakeholder-enabled benefits realisation** (users specify AND help deliver benefits) |
| **6. Rigorous software testing** | → **Ongoing benefits review** (throughout system lifecycle, not just at go-live) |

## Five Principles for Benefits-Oriented Success Factors

1. **Benefits orientation**: The ultimate goal is delivery of clear business benefits, not software
2. **Organisational change**: Benefits come from complementary organizational change, not technology alone
3. **Tailor to context**: No two projects are the same; adapt factors to context
4. **Factors are interdependent**: User involvement × team balance × governance — they interact
5. **Investments have a lifecycle**: Benefits emerge over the operational life of the system, not just during the project
6. **Portfolio focus**: Apply factors across a portfolio, not just single projects

## Key Insight for Analysis
**Benefits rarely spring automatically from technology.** They require proactive management of organizational change:
- Redesign business processes
- Train and empower staff
- Foster appropriate culture
- Establish ongoing benefits review

## How to Use This in Interview Analysis
1. **Assess Norlys's benefits orientation**: Does Norlys define project success as "delivered on time" or as "delivered business value"?
2. **Look for benefits planning**: Are there explicit benefit dependency networks or just vague business cases?
3. **Analyze governance**: Is there a coherent structure linking project teams to business leadership?
4. **Evaluate leadership**: Are senior managers actively leading transformation, or just providing resources?
5. **Check for organizational change**: Are IT projects accompanied by process redesign, training, culture change?
6. **Examine benefits review**: Is there ongoing review after go-live, or does the project end at deployment?
