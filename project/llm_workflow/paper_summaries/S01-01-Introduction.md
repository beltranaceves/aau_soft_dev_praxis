# S01-01: Course Introduction — Systems Development in Praxis

## Source
Course lecture slides (AAU, Systems Development in Praxis)

## Core Purpose
This session sets up the course: students analyze a real-world systems development organization (Norlys) using theories from the course, writing a short paper (4-5 pages) that answers a problem statement.

## Key Context for Analysis
- The exam requires a **theory-directed analysis** of praxis (Norlys)
- Must select ONE theory, apply it systematically, then discuss results with another theory
- Interviews with practitioners are the primary empirical material

## Important Statistics (for motivation/context)
- IT projects have a **73% average cost overrun** (Flyvbjerg et al., 2025)
- IT projects have a "fat tail" of extreme overruns — **553% mean overrun** in the tail
- IT projects are riskier than nuclear waste handling

## Core Framework: Fitzgerald & Howcroft (1998) — 'Soft' vs 'Hard' Dichotomy

### Ontological Level (what is there to know?)
| Position | Belief |
|----------|--------|
| **Relativist** | Multiple realities exist as subjective constructions; reality varies across cultures/languages |
| **Realist** | External world consists of pre-existing hard structures independent of cognition |

### Epistemological Level (what and how can we know?)
| Position | Belief |
|----------|--------|
| **Interpretivist** | No universal truth; understand from participant's frame of reference |
| **Positivist** | World conforms to fixed laws; objectivity, measurement, repeatability |
| **Subjectivist** | Researcher and situation are collapsed; values are central mediators |
| **Objectivist** | Researcher must remain detached; neutral observation |
| **Emic/Insider** | Native/insider's view is the judge of adequacy |
| **Etic/Outsider** | Outside researcher is objective analyst |

### Methodological Level
| Approach | Characteristic |
|----------|---------------|
| **Qualitative** | Determining what exists, thick description, less structured |
| **Quantitative** | Statistical techniques, larger samples, generalization |
| **Exploratory** | Discovering patterns, generating hypotheses |
| **Confirmatory** | Hypothesis testing, theory verification |
| **Induction** | Specific instances → generalizations |
| **Deduction** | General results → specific instances |
| **Field** | Realism of context, less control |
| **Laboratory** | Precise measurement, less natural |
| **Idiographic** | Individual-centered, unique experience |
| **Nomothetic** | Group-centered, general laws |

### Positioning Strategies
| Strategy | Description | Problem |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Supremacism** | One paradigm is universally best | No single approach has answered all questions |
| **Isolationism** | Paradigms are incommensurable | Miss complementary insights |
| **Integrationism** | Integrate into single coherent mode | Presumes an Archimedean vantage point |
| **Pluralism** | Contingent toolbox approach | May descend into anarchy |

## How to Use This in Interview Analysis
- **Position yourself**: When analyzing Norlys interviews, decide your epistemological stance (likely interpretivist, given interview data)
- **Classify statements**: Identify whether practitioners speak from a realist/objectivist stance or a relativist/subjectivist stance
- **Analyze conflicts**: Different positioning strategies can explain disagreements between stakeholders
- **Methodological awareness**: Be explicit about your own research positioning in the paper
