# S11: Agile Distributed Software Development — Enacting Control Through Media and Context

## Source
Persson, J. S., Mathiassen, L., & Aaen, I. (2012). Agile distributed software development: enacting control through media and context. *Information Systems Journal*, 22(6), 411-433.

## Core Idea
How do successful agile distributed software projects enact control through mediated communication and project context? Using **Kirsch's control framework**, the study shows that **formal and informal control can coexist** even in distributed agile teams — contrary to fears that informal (clan-like) control would be impossible at a distance.

## The Challenge
- Agile methods rely on **face-to-face communication** (informal control)
- Distributed teams lack face-to-face interaction
- Prior research questioned whether informal control mechanisms could work in distributed settings
- Yet agile distributed projects succeed — how?

## Control Framework (Kirsch, 2004)

| Element | Formal | Informal |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **Measurement** | Documented goals/behaviors, pre-specified, measurable | Implicit norms/values, evolving goals |
| **Evaluation** | Rules, procedures, written status reports | Socialization, dialogue, discussions |
| **Rewards & Sanctions** | Pay, bonuses, promotion/demotion | Group recognition, peer pressure |
| **Roles & Relationships** | Superior-subordinate dyads | Clan, work group |

## Case Study: The Comapping Project
- **Project**: Web-based collaborative mindmapping tool
- **Team**: Danish firm (Area9) + Russian R&D provider
- **Distribution**: Across geography, culture, and organizations (2-hour time zone difference)
- **Method**: Hybrid of Scrum and XP (sprints, backlogs, continuous integration, code reviews)
- **Data**: 10 observed e-conferences (audio + video recorded) + 11 interviews
- **Success**: Product launched, investment from Fortune500 company

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Both formal AND informal control enacted through mediated communication
| Control Element | In Project Context (interviews) | In Mediated Communication (e-conferences) |
|----------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Measurement | Both formal and informal | Formal: goal specification in mindmap; Informal: evolving goals |
| Evaluation | Both formal and informal | Formal: documented reviews; Informal: expectation negotiation |
| Rewards/Sanctions | Limited (ownership-based) | Informal: group recognition + peer pressure only |
| Roles/Relationships | Mostly informal (clan/work group) | Overwhelmingly informal (clan + work group) |

### Finding 2: Clan-like control persisted despite distribution
- **Contrary to expectations**: Harris et al. (2009) argued clan control would be difficult when interaction is mediated and cross-organizational
- **Comapping showed**: Clan control was pervasively observable both in context and mediated exchanges
- **Why it worked**: Long-term relationships, high trust, high goal congruence, "first get the right people on the bus"

### Finding 3: The collaborative tool (Comapping mindmap) enabled formal measurement without destroying informality
- The mindmap served as a **shared backlog** — goals were documented and specified formally
- But this formalization was embedded in **informal relationships** (clan)
- The tool provided visual cues alternative to face-to-face whiteboard (cursor positions, node manipulation)
- **Result**: Formal controls didn't feel like bureaucratic impediments to agile values

### Finding 4: Different participants emphasized different controls
- **Manager**: Frequent communication as most crucial; "if no exchanges for a week, little work done"
- **Marketer**: "Freedom with responsibility"; trusted developers, focused on strategy
- **Developer**: Preferred face-to-face but didn't consider it necessary between manager and himself

## Practical Implications
1. Communication technologies can **significantly support** distributed agile practices
2. Formal measurement/evaluation controls can be enacted through collaborative tools (shared backlogs, task boards)
3. These formal controls don't undermine informal clan relationships — they can coexist
4. Agile principles (trust, right people, mutual adjustment) can work across distance if supported appropriately
5. **Emergent outcome control**: Continuing corrective actions with evolving standards by multiple stakeholders

## How to Use This in Interview Analysis
1. **Analyze control mechanisms at Norlys**: How is control exercised? Formally (KPIs, documented goals) or informally (trust, clan)?
2. **Examine distributed vs. co-located**: Does Norlys have distributed teams? How does distance affect control?
3. **Look at tools**: What collaborative tools does Norlys use? Do they support or hinder informal control?
4. **Assess clan characteristics**: Is there evidence of clan-like relationships (mutual dependence, shared goals, trust)?
5. **Identify control tensions**: Where does formal control conflict with agile/informal values?
6. **Use Tables 3 & 4**: Compare context vs. mediated communication patterns at Norlys
