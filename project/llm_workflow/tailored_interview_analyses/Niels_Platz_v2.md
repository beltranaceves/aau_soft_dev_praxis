## 2. Weinberg's Overstructured Management

### 2.1 Evidence table

| Code | Status | Strongest passage |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **DEAL-EA** | ✓ (strong) | *"We started with behavior: trying to create a common agreement of what we are working on."* "We have educated our line managers… forcing them into a line where they… become servant leaders." "Some teams need to be held in the hand a bit more." |
| **DEAL-DL** | ✗ (absent in Niels) | Niels never attributes outcomes to task difficulty or bad luck. The only DEAL-DL attribution in the entire transcript is the *removed manager* (inferred, not first-person), where a new manager "fired all the Scrum Masters, and things stopped" because the role "did not know what their customers wanted" — the manager's failure was framed as a behavioural fault, not luck. |
| **Anti-Sequence** | ✓ (strong) | *"You can take revolutions… or you can take evolutions, which is what we are doing here."* "Q Planning is… a necessity where we are right now. But it is not something I want in the future." "You can start by doing an organizational transformation, which always messes up, because you start by just putting people in a new box, and they will act as they did before." "The TPM role is an antipattern of huge proportions, but it is a stepping stone toward something better." |
| **Anti-Modularization** | ✓ (strong, anchored on the Scrum Master case) | *"Then a new manager came in and fired all the Scrum Masters, and things stopped. They created a new application called the [Guba Super Tool], but they did not know what their customers wanted… That manager has now been removed… they saw that it simply costs too much to remove the catalyst in the teams."* "You do not necessarily need Scrum Masters as a title. You need champions of Agile who continue when things get hard." |
| **Anti-Choice** | ✓ (strong) | *"You need to do all of them. This is a terrible thing, because first you need upper management support. If you do not have that, it will fail. Doing it bottom up will at some point hit a Tayloristic way of thinking… I think you need to work on all different levels."* Rejection of binary "bottom-up vs. top-down" framing. |
| **Anti-Iteration** | ✓ (strong) | *"Theory is super important, but let it hit the real world, and let the real world have a veto right."* Repeated ceremonies (some teams' retrospectives) are *not* a guarantee of progress; the world can overrule a plan. "We need to be aware that each change needs to give value, not just break things." |
| **Anti-Recursion** | ✓ (strong) | *"As Agile coaches, we need to be more extreme, otherwise it will not fall through and they will not see the value."* Niels works between teams, line managers, and the apex. He reads outside software. "I have worked with it in teams… I started by coding, and now I am coding with people." |

**Net assessment:** The single interview contains *the* richest anti-overstructured evidence base in the entire data set. Every fallacy receives at least one direct rejection, with the Sequence Fallacy rejected most explicitly (in multiple places) and the Modularization Fallacy rejected by the most vivid single case (the removed manager). DEAL-DL is notable by its absence; Niels is a textbook *successful* manager in Weinberg's terms.

## 3. Hyper-Learning

### 3.1 Evidence table

| Routine | Status | Strongest passage |
|---------|--------|-------------------|
| **DGK — Distributed Gate-Keeping** | ✓ (strong) | *"Some teams need to be held in the hand a bit more, replacing who they had to decide before with somebody else telling them what to do."* The agile coach, scrum masters, and line managers are all described as acting as gate-keepers to the *next* team maturity. Niels himself scans for what the business needs and what managers can absorb. |
| **BEK — Brokering External Knowledge** | ✓ (strong) | The Telia / Norlys cultural integration is the most explicit instance: *"You took an entire company outside that has a totally different way of working, [Telia], and introduced it into Norlys, with [Myrna] as a partner. That is three different ways of working."* Niels himself brokers between Telia's performance-driven culture and Norlys's customer-owned cooperative model. |
| **SDR — Simple Design Rules** | ✓ (strong) | *"Q Planning… candidate list that is extracted from [unclear]. It is locked 14 days before."* "We use T-shirt sizing." Story points, confidence votes, the "picture" heuristic — all are codified decision rules. *"When I asked him: 'Can a large be done in 14 days?' He said no. Then it is not a story."* |
| **PN — Peer Networks** | ✓ (moderate) | *"Code reviews are both good and bad. They give value in two ways: the inexperienced person learns, and the experienced person teaches."* Q Planning is also a horizontal cross-team forum. |
| **PA — Parallel Ambidexterity** | ✓ (strong) | *"We have an event that I am in charge of called Q Planning… The teams have an innovation and planning sprint. They do their work, then we meet around it."* Exploration (innovation and planning sprint) and exploitation (delivery sprints) coexist within the same unit. *"If you find something right now that you need to look into, then do that. Why wait for two and a half months?"* (paraphrased from the same theme in Eva-Maria's interview; here Niels implies it through the IP sprint framing). |

**Net assessment:** All four routines are present. Norlys is the most explicit example of *Parallel Ambidexterity* in the data set: the innovation and planning sprint is built into the rhythm precisely so that exploration does not have to wait for the next delivery cycle. The Brokering routine is the *organizational* one (Telia + Norlys), while the more granular DGK and SDR routines are enacted at the team level.

| **P-RAT — Pull to Rationalize** | ✓ (strong) | The story-point mandate is the clearest case: finance requires a standardized output for every story. *"Adding story points at the end is not Agile methodology."* |

**Net assessment:** Mintzberg's framework paints Norlys as a *hybrid in transition* between Machine and Innovative (Adhocracy) configurations. The technostructure (finance) is still pulling to rationalize. The operating core and apex are pulling toward an innovative/adhocratic state through P-COL and P-PRO. The middle line is the principal *resistance* — exactly the configuration the framework would predict.

## 5. Cross-Theory Patterns

The interview is unusual in that the same passages frequently illustrate two or even all three theories at once. The four richest such patterns:

1. **The Scrum Master removal case** is the *anchor case* for the entire paper. It illustrates:
   - *Weinberg:* Modularization Fallacy (manager treated the role as a swappable module)
   - *Hyper-learning:* Loss of *Distributed Gate-Keeping* and a node in the *Peer Network* (Scrum Masters were "the catalyst in the teams")
   - *Mintzberg:* Middle Line (the new manager) imposed direct supervision and dismantled a liaison device; *also* P-COL was reversed by the middle line

2. **The story points tension with finance** illustrates:
   - *Weinberg:* Iteration Fallacy (story points as a routine becoming a cliché at end-of-story); also DEAL-DL avoidance on Niels's side
   - *Hyper-learning:* SDR (the simple rule of relative estimation) was *imposed* by a technostructure pull, but *internalized* by teams ("they created their own poker planning cards")
   - *Mintzberg:* P-RAT (Pull to Rationalize) met P-PRO (Pull to Professionalize) and, in this case, P-PRO won

3. **The IP sprint** illustrates:
   - *Weinberg:* Anti-Sequence (don't repeat the same delivery cycle; reserve a separate slot for exploration)
   - *Hyper-learning:* Parallel Ambidexterity (exploration and exploitation in the same unit)
   - *Mintzberg:* Support-staff (Niels) and operating-core (teams) joint ownership of innovation

4. **The "evolution, not revolution" stance** illustrates:
   - *Weinberg:* Anti-Sequence (no fixed roadmap)
   - *Hyper-learning:* Each "stepping stone" is a *simple design rule*; together they form a *routine* that the organization can iterate on
   - *Mintzberg:* Post-merger diversified configuration is being *actively* dragged toward innovative/adhocratic through Q Planning and agile-coach liaison devices — i.e. a deliberate anti-P-BAL strategy

## 7. Notes for the Main Paper

- Niels is the only interviewee who voices **all five** anti-fallacies explicitly. The other four interviewees can corroborate, but they rarely *introduce* the rejection in their own words.
- The Scrum Master case carries the paper's argumentative weight. It is the single narrative that maps cleanly onto all three theories.
- Niels's data is heavily *advocate* in tone. The paper should already flag (and does) the positive-bias risk. The Birgitte, Kasper, and Eva-Maria interviews supply useful counter-checks (especially on the rigidity of the QBR model, the persistence of finance as technostructure, and the pain of the middle line).
