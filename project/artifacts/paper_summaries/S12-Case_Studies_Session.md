### 4. Case Study 2: Cross-Cultural Knowledge Sharing (Persson, 2013)
**Research approach**: Interpretive single case study
**Setting**: Large IT integration project in Nordic financial sector; Indian outsourcing provider
**Sub-project**: 11 persons (3 Indian, 5 Danish developers)
**Theoretical framework**: Communities of Practice (Wenger)

**14 Indicators of Communities of Practice** (used as coding scheme):
1. Sustained mutual relationships
2. Shared ways of engaging together
3. Rapid flow of information
4. Conversations/interactions are ongoing
5. Quick setup of problems
6. Overlap in who belongs
7. Knowing what others know, do, and contribute
8. Mutually defining identities
9. Ability to assess actions and products
10. Specific tools, representations, artifacts
11. Lore, stories, jokes, laughter
12. Jargon and shortcuts to communication
13. Styles displaying membership
14. Shared discourse and perspective

**Key Finding**: After 10½ months of co-location, Indian developers reported being "one among them" — all 14 indicators were present, compared to very few at 2 weeks.

**Strategies for successful co-location**:
- Shared office
- Shared responsibility for tasks and problems
- Shared prioritization of team spirit
- A champion of social integration

### 6. Argumentation Rules (Weston, 2017)
General rules:
1. Resolve premises and conclusion
2. Unfold ideas in natural order
3. Start from reliable premises
4. Be concrete and concise
5. Build on substance, not overtone
6. Use consistent terms

Common fallacies to avoid:
- *Ad ignorantiam* (true by absence of falsification)
- *Affirming the consequent*
- *Begging the question*
- *Equivocation*
- *False dilemma*
