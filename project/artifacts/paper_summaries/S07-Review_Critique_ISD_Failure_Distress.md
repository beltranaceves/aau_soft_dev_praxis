# S07: Review and Critique of ISD Project Failure Literature — Exploring Project Distress

## Source
Baghizadeh, Z., Cecez-Kecmanovic, D., & Schlagwein, D. (2020). Review and critique of the information systems development project failure literature: An argument for exploring information systems development project distress. *Journal of Information Technology*, 35(2), 123-142.

## Core Idea
ISD project failure research has been heavily focused on **failure as an end state**. The authors argue we should instead study **ISD project distress** — the dynamic, fluid constellation of critical problems that occur **before** a project fails. By the time a project is a "failure," it's too late to intervene.

## Three Perspectives on ISD Project Failure

| Perspective | Conceptualization | Focus | Weakness |
|------------|-----------------|-------|----------|
| **1. Rationalist** | Failure as objective, measurable outcome (over budget, late, poor quality) | Identify causal factors that lead to failure | Ignores context; assumes linear causality; oversimplifies |
| **2. Process** | Failure as emergent from dynamic socio-technical processes | Understand project flaws, escalation, misalignment | Still ultimately outcome-focused |
| **3. Narrative/Social Constructivist** | Failure as socially constructed through stakeholder interpretations | How meaning is attributed to outcomes | Lacks generalizability; focuses on post-hoc narratives |

**Common weakness across all three**: They all focus on failure as an **end state** — neglecting the problematic situations arising **during** the project.

## The Key Innovation: ISD Project Distress

### Definition
> "A harmful project condition involving dynamic and fluid constellation of critical problems that are difficult to identify, understand and resolve."

### Key Properties
- **Dynamic** — constantly changing
- **Emergent** — arises from interactions within the project
- **Not easily detectable** — may be invisible to standard metrics
- **Not necessarily leading to failure** — distress can be resolved if identified and managed

### Why This Matters
Traditional failure research asks: "Why did this project fail?" (post-mortem)
Distress research asks: "What critical problems are emerging RIGHT NOW that threaten the project?" (real-time intervention)

## Proposed Framework: Sensemaking (Weick, 1995)
The authors propose applying sensemaking to understand how project participants recognize, interpret, and respond to distress:
- **Enactment**: How do participants create the conditions they then respond to?
- **Selection**: How do they interpret ambiguous cues of distress?
- **Retention**: How do past experiences shape responses to current distress?

## Research Method
- **Hermeneutic literature review** (interpretive, iterative)
- 321 sources from IS and project management literature
- Databases: EBSCO, ScienceDirect, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar
- Keywords: ISD project failure, success, evaluation, turnaround, escalation, process flaws

## How to Use This in Interview Analysis
1. **Identify distress signals**: In Norlys interviews, look for descriptions of "critical problems" that are dynamic, emergent, and hard to pin down — not yet failures, but problematic
2. **Assess detection capabilities**: Does Norlys have mechanisms to detect distress early, or do they only notice when failure is imminent?
3. **Analyze sensemaking**: How do different stakeholders interpret ambiguous situations? Do they make sense of problems differently?
4. **Compare perspectives**: Which perspective (rationalist, process, narrative) best describes how Norlys talks about project problems?
5. **Apply the distress concept**: Instead of asking "did this project succeed/fail?", ask "what distress was present and how was it handled?"
6. **Look for escalation**: Evidence of escalating commitment to failing courses of action
