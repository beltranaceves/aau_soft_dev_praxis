## 2. Weinberg's Overstructured Management

### 2.1 Evidence table

| Code | Status | Strongest passage |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **DEAL-EA** | ✓ (moderate) | *"My main role is to remove obstacles for the developers and they don't always say, oh, this is an issue. Help me with this."* Attributes obstacles to the team's *effort* in speaking up, not to external circumstance. *"We just have to find out what the right thing is."* The "remaining five percent just never really get found out so we end up almost finishing a task" — Eva-Maria treats this as an effort problem, not a difficulty problem. |
| **DEAL-DL** | — (no direct evidence) | Eva-Maria does not blame difficulty or luck for anything. |

| **Anti-Modularization** | — (no direct evidence) | She does not speak of people-as-modules. Her role is the opposite: deeply relational. |

| **Anti-Recursion** | ✓ (strong) | The "guardrail" framing is the literal embodiment of anti-recursion: *"I have to be the one that says: are we sure this is what we want to do? **Net assessment:** Eva-Maria's *non-technical* background turns out to be the *theoretical* asset for the paper. She is the only interviewee who articulates the *guardrail* function explicitly — a recursion-fallacy antidote at the team level. Her Anti-Choice and Anti-Iteration evidence is also the most plainly stated in the data set.

## 3. Hyper-Learning

### 3.1 Evidence table

| Routine | Status | Strongest passage |
|---------|--------|-------------------|
| **DGK — Distributed Gate-Keeping** | ✓ (strong) | *"My main role is to remove obstacles for the developers… I try to stay informed. So I know as well that, okay, we're working on this part right now, and in the future, we will have… these issues."* Eva-Maria is the gate-keeper who absorbs signals that the developers do not raise as tickets. *"All the tasks that are coming in, it's not… I don't have the PO's job, so it's not my responsibility to get information to the team, but I like to stay informed."* The Scrum Master role is itself a distributed gate-keeping function. |
| **BEK — Brokering External Knowledge** | — (no direct evidence) | Eva-Maria's interview does not feature cross-organizational knowledge brokering. The Telia broker is Niels and Rasmus. |
| **SDR — Simple Design Rules** | ✓ (moderate) | The drop of sprint review is itself a *simple rule change*: when the context (18 services) makes the rule useless, the rule gets removed. The dailies and the *calendar-blocked* knowledge-sharing sessions are also codified rules. *"We have them in the calendar like once a month or we just say this time is for knowledge sharing."* |
| **PN — Peer Networks** | ✓ (strong) | *"We do have knowledge sharing sessions. So, we have them in the calendar like once a month or we just say this time is for knowledge sharing but if something pops up like if we worked something more on AI for instance then we would just say okay on Tuesday you will present what you found out."* The peer network is the team's *primary* learning mechanism. |
| **PA — Parallel Ambidexterity** | ✓ (strong) | The IP sprint is the clearest single example in the data set: *"In the process that sprint is meant to be the sprint where you innovate and get smarter and stuff but I cheat. And I think it's more important that if you find something right now that you need to look into, then do that. Why wait for two and a half months?"* The sprint is the *formal* slot for exploration; her "cheating" — innovation *within* a delivery sprint — is *informal* parallel ambidexterity. |

**Net assessment:** Eva-Maria is the *parallel ambidexterity* anchor. The IP sprint is the most clearly named *exploration-and-exploitation-in-same-unit* mechanism in the data. She also brings a strong DGK evidence through the listening role and a strong PN evidence through the knowledge-sharing sessions.

## 5. Cross-Theory Patterns

Three patterns are particularly informative:

1. **The IP sprint** (PA × Anti-Sequence × P-PRO). Eva-Maria "cheats" by exploring within a delivery sprint. This is *Parallel Ambidexterity* (Lyytinen), the explicit rejection of a fixed sequence (Weinberg), and the *operating core's* professional pull (P-PRO). The same innovation is described in three different theoretical registers.

2. **The "guardrail" role** (Anti-Recursion × DGK × SS). The Scrum Master is the team's *support-staff liaison* (Mintzberg), the *distributed gate-keeper* who listens for problems (Lyytinen), and the *recursion-fallacy antidote* who asks "are we sure this is what we want to do?" (Weinberg). Her non-IT background is not a deficit; it is the *enabling condition* for the anti-recursion function.

3. **The dropped sprint review** (Anti-Sequence × SDR × P-COL). Dropping a Scrum ceremony is *not* anti-agile; it is a *simple design rule* (Lyytinen) that the team updated. The team is *not* iterating the same ceremony (Anti-Iteration in Weinberg's sense); they *changed* the rule. From Mintzberg, this is a micro-act of horizontal *collaboration* (P-COL) — the team adjusts its own coordination mechanism.

## 7. Notes for the Main Paper

- The IP sprint is the *single best* fieldstone for the Lyytinen hyper-learning claim of *parallel ambidexterity*. The paper should anchor that claim here, not in Niels's interview.
- The "guardrail" framing is the single best fieldstone for Weinberg's Anti-Recursion. It is also the most concise statement of *why a non-IT Scrum Master adds value* — a nice counter-intuitive data point.
- Eva-Maria's data is a *non-advocate* source (she is closer to the operating core and openly critical of some practices, e.g. dropping reviews, the speed/quality trade-off, the IT-business mismatch). This is a useful counter-weight to Niels's positive bias.
- Her account of the IT-business mismatch ("I would like for the business to work in a more Agile way") is the strongest *Mintzberg middle-line critique* in the data set. It is consistent with Niels's "management is the hard part" but adds a *specific structural* reading: the apex-and-IT pull to innovate is undermined by the *business side* (often the SA in diversified post-merger settings) that is not on the same cadence.
